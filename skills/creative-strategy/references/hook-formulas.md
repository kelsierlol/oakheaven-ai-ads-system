# Hook Formulas — Reference File

Reference file for the Ad Script & Storyboard Generator. When generating hooks, select from these named types. Three hooks per script = three different named types.

---

## How to Use This File

1. Read the persona's awareness level and the angle from the creative strategy map.
2. Check the "Best For" column to narrow your options.
3. Select 3 different hook types that fit the context.
4. Name the hook type explicitly in your output before writing the hook.
5. Every hook must also contain a **traffic qualifier** (a niche-specific term, price point, role, or life-situation detail that filters out non-ICP viewers).

---

## Hook Types

### 1. Catalyst Hook

**Definition:** Uses a recent event, new discovery, regulatory change, or specific condition as the reason the offer is relevant RIGHT NOW. It feels like news, not an ad.

**Best For:** Any awareness level. Particularly strong when you have a timely angle — a new study, a policy change, a market shift, a seasonal trigger.

**Structure:** "[Audience qualifier] now have the opportunity to [outcome] because of [recent catalyst]."

**Examples:**
- "Florida residents now have the opportunity to get a new roof for $59/month because of a new state financing programme."
- "AI has now made it possible for solo founders to run a faceless YouTube channel that generates $5k/month — without recording a single video."
- "After the latest Meta algorithm update, e-commerce brands spending $10k+/month on ads are seeing their CPAs drop by 30% — but only if they do this one thing."

**Why it works:** Provides a specific, external justification for why this matters now. Overcomes scepticism and banner blindness because it feels timely and newsworthy.

---

### 2. Transformation Hook

**Definition:** Leads with a specific before/after in a defined timeframe. Shows the gap between where they are and where they could be.

**Best For:** Problem-Aware and Solution-Aware audiences. Needs proof to land — pair with a specific result.

**Structure:** "I went from [specific before state] to [specific after state] in [timeframe]."

**Examples:**
- "I went from spending $10k/month to break even on ads to spending $100k/month and printing money — in 90 days."
- "6 months ago I was editing videos until 2am. Now I spend $30 per video and my channel makes $8k/month."
- "She went from 3 clients at $2k/month to 12 clients at $5k/month. The only thing she changed was this."

**Why it works:** Specific numbers + specific timeframe = believable. Vague transformations ("I changed my life") get ignored.

---

### 3. Contrarian Hook

**Definition:** Challenges a common belief the audience holds. Says something that makes them stop and think "wait, what?"

**Best For:** Solution-Aware audiences who have tried other approaches and are stuck. Bold and polarising — filters hard.

**Structure:** "[Common belief everyone holds] is wrong. Here's why."

**Examples:**
- "Personal brands that are built on giving value are dead."
- "The reason your ads aren't working has nothing to do with your targeting."
- "Stop doing crunches if you want to lose belly fat."

**Why it works:** Pattern interrupt through cognitive dissonance. The audience needs to resolve the contradiction, so they keep watching.

---

### 4. Proof Lead Hook

**Definition:** Opens with a specific result, number, or case study. Leads with evidence, not a claim.

**Best For:** Product-Aware audiences. Also strong for Solution-Aware when the proof is dramatic enough to stop the scroll.

**Structure:** "[Specific person/brand] achieved [specific result] using [mechanism hint]."

**Examples:**
- "We helped Colgate turn one winning ad that generated $100k into 10 winning ads that generated $1.3 million."
- "847 e-commerce brands have used this system to cut their CAC by 40% in 30 days."
- "Hey, e-commerce marketing managers — eight and nine figure brands don't burn ad spend on random creatives. They do this."

**Why it works:** Proof is the #1 differentiator in ads. Specific numbers build instant credibility. The audience self-selects: "I want that result."

---

### 5. Relatable Moment Hook

**Definition:** Describes a situation the persona is currently living through. The audience recognises their own life and thinks "that's me."

**Best For:** Unaware and Problem-Aware audiences. The persona doesn't need to know they have a "problem" — they just need to recognise the feeling.

**Structure:** "[Vivid description of a moment in their daily life that captures the pain/frustration]."

**Examples:**
- "Every morning at 3am, wide awake for no reason. Staring at the ceiling. Again."
- "You open Ads Manager, see the CPAs climbing, and that sinking feeling hits — another week of burning budget."
- "You're sitting at your kid's birthday party, and someone takes a photo, and you think 'I used to look so much better.'"

**Why it works:** Recognition is the most powerful hook mechanism for cold traffic. They're not responding to a claim — they're responding to their own experience reflected back at them.

---

### 6. Missed Step Hook

**Definition:** "You're doing everything right except this one thing." Creates curiosity about the specific gap.

**Best For:** Solution-Aware audiences. They've tried things, they're doing the work, but something isn't clicking.

**Structure:** "You're doing [correct things] but missing [one specific thing] — and that's why [outcome isn't happening]."

**Examples:**
- "You're testing more hooks, more formats, more audiences — but your CAC keeps climbing. The problem isn't volume. It's this."
- "You're eating clean, working out 4x a week, and still not losing weight. It's not discipline. It's one hormone you've never tested."
- "The difference between a multi-seven figure and an eight-figure brand isn't that they're testing more creative — it's that they're testing more of the RIGHT creative."

**Why it works:** Validates their effort (they're not lazy or stupid), then creates a curiosity gap about the one thing they're missing. The open loop is built into the hook.

---

### 7. Identity Hook

**Definition:** Speaks directly to who they are by role, stage, or situation. Qualifies the traffic immediately by naming the audience.

**Best For:** Any awareness level. Particularly strong for B2B or niche audiences where the identity is a badge they wear.

**Structure:** "[Specific identity/role/stage]: [statement that only matters to them]."

**Examples:**
- "E-commerce brands doing $500k-$2M per year: if your ads have hit a wall, this is for you."
- "Agency owners running paid media for 5+ clients: there's a reason your best month is always followed by your worst."
- "If you're running a faceless YouTube channel on a $30/video editing budget — stop and watch this."

**Why it works:** The qualifier IS the hook. Non-ICP viewers self-select out immediately. Meta's pixel gets a clean signal about who's engaging.

---

### 8. Misconception Hook

**Definition:** Names something the audience believes is true and reveals it's wrong. Different from Contrarian — this is about correcting a specific, widely-held misconception, not being provocative.

**Best For:** Problem-Aware and Solution-Aware audiences. Education-first play that reframes their understanding.

**Structure:** "You think [common belief]. But it's actually [surprising truth]."

**Examples:**
- "You think your back pain is from bad posture, but it's not. It's actually caused by a tiny, forgotten muscle in your hip."
- "That expensive anti-ageing cream you're using? It probably contains an ingredient that's actually accelerating your skin's ageing."
- "Most people think scaling ads means spending more money. It actually means spending the same money on more different things."

**Why it works:** Reframes the problem in a way that makes their current approach feel insufficient. Creates an education gap the ad fills.

---

### 9. Symptom Hook

**Definition:** Describes a symptom without naming the problem. Lets the audience self-identify based on what they're experiencing, not what they've diagnosed.

**Best For:** Unaware audiences. They don't know they have "this problem" — they just know something feels off.

**Structure:** "[Vivid symptom description]. [Hint that there's a hidden cause]."

**Examples:**
- "Tired all the time, brain foggy by 2pm, and no amount of coffee fixes it. There's a reason — and it's not what you think."
- "Your YouTube channel gets views but zero subscribers. Your content isn't the problem."
- "The hidden danger in your daily routine that's costing you $500/month and you don't even know it."

**Why it works:** Meets the audience at their level of awareness. They haven't labelled the problem yet — so you describe the symptom they're living with.

---

### 10. Pattern Interrupt Hook

**Definition:** Says or shows something structurally unexpected that breaks the scroll pattern. Can be visual, verbal, or both.

**Best For:** Any awareness level. High stop rate. Needs a strong lead immediately after to hold attention — pattern interrupts grab but don't hold on their own.

**Structure:** [Unexpected statement, visual, or format break] + [immediate qualifier or context].

**Examples:**
- "If your banana is not 6 inches..." (food brand — pattern interrupt through innuendo that resolves innocently)
- "I spent $40,000 on ads last month. And I'm about to show you every single number." (Unexpected transparency)
- "DELETE all your ads. Right now." (Provocative command that demands explanation)

**Why it works:** Breaks the unconscious scroll. But WARNING: Pattern interrupts without qualifiers attract everyone, not just ICP. Always pair with a qualifier in the lead or the first 5 seconds of the body.

## Quick Selection Matrix

| Awareness Level | Recommended Hook Types | Avoid |
|-----------------|----------------------|-------|
| Unaware | Relatable Moment, Symptom, Catalyst, Pattern Interrupt | Proof Lead (they don't care about results for a problem they haven't identified) |
| Problem-Aware | Relatable Moment, Misconception, Missed Step, Identity | Symptom (they already know the problem — naming the symptom is redundant) |
| Solution-Aware | Contrarian, Missed Step, Misconception, Transformation | Symptom, Relatable Moment (too basic — they've moved past recognition) |
| Product-Aware | Proof Lead, Identity, Transformation, Catalyst | Misconception (they already understand — proof is what they need) |
| Most Aware | Proof Lead, Catalyst, Identity | Most types are too long for this audience. Keep hooks short and direct. |
