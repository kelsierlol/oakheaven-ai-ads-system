## The Four Variables That Create Entity Diversity (Andromeda Framework)

When building the strategy map, these are the four variables Meta's Andromeda system reads to assign Entity IDs, in order of importance:

### Variable 1: Angle (Most Important)
The specific belief, pain, desire, or emotional trigger the ad speaks to. This is the "big idea." Two ads with completely different visuals, actors, and formats but the same underlying message ("lose weight with healthy recipes") get similar Entity IDs and reach the same audience pool.

Genuine angle diversity means ads that communicate fundamentally different messages to different customer personas. Each distinct angle is a separate creative concept that Andromeda indexes separately and retrieves for a different person.

Example of genuinely different angles for a weight loss product:
- "Lose weight by eating more, not less" (anti-deprivation)
- "The reason you can't lose weight after 50 is hormonal, not caloric" (menopause mechanism)
- "You're exhausted because of what you're eating, not how much you're sleeping" (energy/fatigue)
- "I tried every diet. This is the first thing that actually worked." (frustrated skeptic)
- "I want to keep up with my grandkids" (quality of life / mobility)

These are five different angles. Andromeda assigns them five different Entity IDs and retrieves them for five different people.

### Variable 2: Format
The structural container for the message. Andromeda reads format as a primary signal because it determines which placement the ad is eligible for and how it is experienced.

Genuinely different formats:
- Static image (single)
- Carousel
- Short-form video (under 30 seconds)
- Long-form video (60 seconds+)
- Talking-head / direct spokesperson
- UGC / selfie-style
- Testimonial / case study
- Educational / explainer
- Reaction video
- Product demo / unboxing
- Text-heavy / quote card

Moving from a static food image to a talking-head video creates a different Entity ID even if the angle is the same. Format diversity is the second most powerful lever after angle.

### Variable 3: Persona
The specific customer segment the ad is visually and tonally designed for. This is NOT the targeting setting in Ads Manager. It is who the ad looks and feels like it was made for.

Persona signals Andromeda reads:
- Age, gender, and appearance of the person in the ad
- Setting and lifestyle context (kitchen, gym, office, outdoors)
- Language register and tone (formal, casual, medical, conversational)
- Specific life situation referenced in the hook

Two ads with the same angle but different personas (55-year-old woman talking about menopause vs. 35-year-old woman talking about post-pregnancy weight) get different Entity IDs and reach different people.

### Variable 4: Actor/Spokesperson
The person delivering the message is a strong visual signal Andromeda reads independently of the script. Same script, different face/age/energy = meaningfully different Entity ID.

Actor selection principles:
- Pick clear archetypes. The more stereotypical the archetype, the more coherent the Entity ID.
- "Wise older doctor", "relatable mum in kitchen", "energetic fitness woman in her 40s" each unlock different audience segments.
- Match the archetype to the persona, not to the brand owner.

### Entity ID Creation Tiers

**Tier 1 (Almost certain to create a new Entity ID):**
1. New concept with a new pain point
2. New persona or character
3. New hook format (static to video, testimonial to educational)
4. New opening scene (first 3 seconds of video)
5. New thumbnail on an existing video

**Tier 2 (Likely to create a meaningfully different Entity ID):**
1. New headline with a different emotional angle
2. New primary text with a different story or mechanism
3. New setting or visual context
4. New caption angle on the same static image

**Tier 3 (May create a minor variation):**
1. New aspect ratio or format
2. New music or audio
3. New CTA or button text

**Tier 4 (Will NOT create a new Entity ID):**
1. Changing text style
2. Adding/removing a logo
3. Adjusting brightness, contrast, or color grading
4. Cropping or resizing
5. Changing caption punctuation or emoji

**The bottom-line test:** If a stranger looked at your new ad and your old ad side by side, would they say they are about the same thing? If yes, you have not created a new Entity ID.

## Awareness Levels (Eugene Schwartz Framework)

| Level | Who They Are | What The Ad Does | Hook Style | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unaware | Has symptoms but has not identified the problem | Interrupt with a relatable moment or situation | Lead with a symptom or moment, NOT a problem label | "Every morning at 3am, wide awake for no reason..." |
| Problem-Aware | Knows the problem, does not know solutions exist | Validate the problem, introduce the possibility of a solution | Call out the problem, empathize, hint at a solution | "If you've been struggling to lose weight after 50, it's not your fault..." |
| Solution-Aware | Knows solutions exist, has not found the right one | Differentiate the mechanism, address why other solutions failed | Lead with why other approaches fail, introduce your unique mechanism | "Most diets fail because they ignore the one hormone that controls fat storage after menopause..." |
| Product-Aware | Knows your product, has not bought | Overcome objections, social proof, risk reduction | Lead with proof, address specific hesitations | "40,000 women have already tried this. Here's what happened..." |
| Most Aware | Ready to buy, just needs a push | Make it easy, reinforce the decision, urgency | Direct offer, testimonial, urgency, limited-time incentive | "Ready to start? Join now and get 20% off your first month." |

**Key strategic insight:** If all ads in the strategy map start with "Struggling with X?", you are ONLY speaking to the Problem-Aware market. The Unaware market is the largest segment and the cheapest to reach, but requires a completely different hook style.

**Awareness distribution check:** After completing the strategy map, count how many hooks target each awareness level. If more than 60% target a single level, the strategy has an awareness diversity problem. Flag it.

## The Nine Tagging Dimensions (Detailed Definitions)

Use these definitions during Step 2 (First-Pass Tagging and Extraction).

### 1. Pains
What specifically hurts, frustrates, or bothers them? Not surface-level ("wants to lose weight") but deep and specific ("has gained 20 pounds since menopause and none of her clothes fit anymore"). Tag the emotional weight, not just the category.

### 2. Desires
What do they actually want as an outcome? The dream state. Not the product. "I want to feel like myself again" is different from "I want to lose weight." Tag the emotional destination.

### 3. Current State
What are they currently doing to try to solve this? What is working and what is not working? This reveals where they are in their journey.

### 4. Goals
What concrete targets are they trying to hit? Distinct from desires. Goals are specific and measurable. Desires are emotional. Goals are tangible.

### 5. Barriers and Objections
What is stopping them from buying or acting? Practical barriers AND psychological barriers.

### 6. FAQs
What questions do they keep asking? Recurring questions signal confusion, uncertainty, or unresolved concerns. Often objections in question form.

### 7. Triggers
What event, moment, or experience pushed them from passive awareness to active search? These are hook gold. The trigger is often the opening scene of the ad.

### 8. Trends
Patterns in behavior, market shifts, seasonal factors, or recurring themes across multiple data sources.

### 9. Customer Language
Exact phrases, metaphors, and expressions customers use to describe their situation. Tag VERBATIM. Do not paraphrase. The raw language IS the insight.

## Persona Scoring Framework

### Cold Traffic Convertibility Score (1-10)

| Sub-Factor | Weight | Score 1-10 Scale |
|---|---|---|
| Urgency | 40% | 1 = "nice to have someday" ... 10 = "need to fix this NOW or face serious consequences" |
| Awareness Level | 30% | 1 = completely unaware, requires extensive education ... 10 = already solution/product-aware, knows what they need |
| Skepticism Level (inverse) | 30% | 1 = extremely skeptical, "tried everything", high proof burden ... 10 = open-minded, first-time buyer, low resistance |

**Formula:** (Urgency x 0.4) + (Awareness x 0.3) + (Skepticism_Inverse x 0.3) = Cold Traffic Score

### Market Scalability Score (1-10)

| Sub-Factor | Weight | Score 1-10 Scale |
|---|---|---|
| TAM (Total Addressable Market) | 50% | 1 = extremely niche, tiny market ... 10 = massive addressable population |
| Creative Scalability | 50% | 1 = one-dimensional, messaging exhausted in 3-4 ads ... 10 = deep well of angles, stories, and hooks |

**Formula:** (TAM x 0.5) + (Creative_Scalability x 0.5) = Market Scalability Score

Present both sub-scores alongside the final score so the user can see and challenge the reasoning.
