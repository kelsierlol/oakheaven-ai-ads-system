## Finding 1 — Oakhaven's price anchor is aimed at the wrong comparison ⚠️

The VSL anchors against **in-house payroll**: *"an editor AND a project manager would cost you $5,000 to $10,000 a month… you can hire them for less than one-fourth of that."*

That framing assumes the prospect's alternative is hiring. It isn't. Their alternative is **VidChops at $495/month**, which already includes a dedicated editor, 1–2 day turnaround, and a money-back guarantee — and at **$995** adds the dedicated account manager and unlimited revisions.

If Oakhaven lands at the implied $1,250–$2,500, it sits in EditCrew's band while a functionally similar-sounding offer sells at one-fifth the entry price. The payroll anchor doesn't defend that gap, because the prospect never seriously considered payroll.

**What this means:** either the price comes down toward the $495–$995 band, or the copy must justify a 2–5× premium over VidChops on something other than headcount and turnaround. That justification has to be the mechanism. **This is now the single most important pricing decision in the offer**, and it can't be made until the client confirms the actual price (Q1 of the questionnaire).

## Finding 2 — Every feature claim in Oakhaven's VSL is already table stakes

| Oakhaven claim | Already in market |
|---|---|
| Dedicated project/creative manager | VidChops Pro ($995) and EditCrew (all tiers) |
| 24-hour turnaround | VidChops 1–2 days *guaranteed*; EditCrew 1–2 day first drafts + 24hr revisions |
| 14-day money-back guarantee | VidChops, near-verbatim. EditCrew has a 14-day satisfaction guarantee |
| Two people per account | EditCrew gives **three** — editor, project manager, *and* creative producer |
| "An editing team that sounds like you" | VidChops landing copy, verbatim |

**Not one differentiator in the current VSL survives contact with the market.** The two-person structure is
actively *weaker* than EditCrew's three-person one as a headcount claim.

## Finding 3 — Sophistication question: ANSWERED. Yes, it's sophisticated.

The client flagged this twice and never resolved it. The evidence resolves it:

- Multiple funded competitors running identical time-saved and reliability claims at scale
- Feature parity across the board on turnaround, guarantee, and dedicated staffing
- A price ladder from $495 to $2,450+, meaning buyers are already comparison-shopping tiers
- Prospects in the VOC data who have already bought and been burned

**Consequence:** stage-3 benefit claims ("save 10 hours a week," "never miss a deadline," "24-hour turnaround") are exhausted. They will get scrolled. The VSL v1 (time-saved led, "500+ hours a year") is the **wrong** version to run. **VSL v2 (mechanism-led) is the correct one**, and the client's own instinct to write it was right.

## Finding 4 — The mechanism lane is still open, and it's the only one

Nobody in the swipe set claims **comprehension**. EditCrew's *"plug-and-play process built to save you time"* is the closest thing to a named mechanism and it's a workflow claim, not a quality claim — and it points at *time*, the exhausted benefit.

Nobody says: *your edits are generic because your editor doesn't understand what you mean.*

That is Oakhaven's entire opening. The Content Immersion System — Brand Download → Contextual Editing → Quality Gate — is a real stage-4 mechanism in a stage-3 market. It should carry every ad, and the Quality Gate should be sold as **the thing that catches the error**, not as "you get a project manager" (which is a $995 commodity).

## Finding 5 — The proof gap is worse than it looked

EditCrew runs **named testimonials with numbers** on the landing page. Oakhaven has zero proof assets of any kind. In a sophisticated, burned market, going up against named social proof with an unproven mechanism claim is the weakest possible position. The side-by-side asset moves from "nice to have" to **required before spend**.

---

## Actions this forces

1. **Confirm the real price** before anything else. The entire positioning depends on where it lands relative to $495 / $995 / $1,500.
2. **Kill the payroll anchor** as the primary frame, or demote it. Rebuild the value argument against the $495–$995 subscription alternative.
3. **Run VSL v2 (mechanism), not v1 (time-saved).** Update `context/business.md` messaging priority.
4. **Reposition the PM** from "you get a dedicated project manager" (commodity) to what the Quality Gate *catches* — the wrong graphic, the irrelevant b-roll, the typo — which is the mechanism's payoff, not a staffing line item.
5. **Drop these from the copy entirely** as differentiators: 24-hour turnaround, dedicated PM, flat pricing, no-ghosting, custom graphics, "sounds like you." Use them as table-stakes reassurance *after* the mechanism lands, never as the hook.
6. **Produce the side-by-side** before spend. It is the only asset that proves the one claim nobody else is making.
