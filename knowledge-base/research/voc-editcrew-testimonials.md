## 🔴 Finding: EditCrew's buyers are agencies, not coaches

Of 13 customers: **8 are video production agencies, marketing agencies, or production companies**
(River North Media, Tru Focus Productions, Siskey Productions, Front Runner Films, Viva Media,
Team of Scientists, Youth Lens 360, Business Builders). The rest are a content strategist, an
acupuncture educator, a photography YouTuber, and a church media lead.

**Zero coaches.** None.

At $1,500–$2,450/month EditCrew is selling **white-label editing capacity to agencies**, not
done-for-you content to personal brands. That reframes the competitive map: EditCrew may not be
competing for Oakhaven's stated ICP at all, and the coach/creator segment is likely served by
VidChops' $495 tier.

**Implication to test:** either the coach segment is underserved at the premium end (opportunity), or
it doesn't pay premium prices for editing (which is why nobody sells to it). This is now the most
important open question in the positioning, and it's cheap to answer — it's what the ICP interviews are for.

## Freelancer failure — specific, and better than anything we had

> "It takes a week or two, sometimes longer, just to get a freelancer onboarded. And when I have a project, I need it done in like two days. And then what happens is you don't have a project for a couple of months and you go back to that same person and they are no longer available — and they've already learned your stuff and learned your styles, but they're gone."
— Adriel Hampton

**This is the strongest freelancer quote in the entire VOC bank.** It names a cost nobody advertises against:
you don't just lose the freelancer, you lose the *training investment* you made in them. Oakhaven's Brand
Download answers this directly.

> "I think it's four times the amount just to have somebody be available… 'there's a project gonna start in two days.' Oh, they just pushed it another four days. Oh, it's not till next week. Oh well, too bad, you gotta pay me."
— Ryan Stober, Team of Scientists

> "It's really hard to retain good talent."
— Christopher Dorsano

> "We weren't in a spot where I could hire somebody full time at a rate that I could afford on a regular consistent basis."
— Andrew Cardy

## Pre-purchase objections — rare and valuable
*These are the doubts they name before buying. Closest thing to sales-call objection data we have.*

> "At first, I didn't believe it. I thought, there'd be a company that's gonna send you the stuff back quickly? Are they good editors?… They're gonna give us our first draft in forty-eight hours. That doesn't sound real."
— Dario Otero, Youth Lens 360

> "At first I thought, what if a client finds out this other team is editing the product?"
— Dario Otero

**The white-label shame objection.** Specific to agency buyers, and nobody in the swipe set addresses it.

> "I felt like they weren't going to diminish the quality of my brand… They weren't going to diminish that at all. They would just add to it."
— Tyler Nimmons, Front Runner Films

The fear underneath: **outsourcing degrades your brand.** That's the objection Oakhaven's mechanism exists to answer.

## 🎯 The mechanism, validated by a customer's own words

> "It only took after like the second one to really get the vibe and to get the groove. So the ability to pick up the style and to subliminally find what we wanted the style to be was just great… Everything I conceptualized and visualized it being was exactly what we got. It felt fresh almost every single video. It's almost like having someone in-house knowing exactly what we wanted."
— Conor Hildebrandt, Viva Media

This is the Content Immersion promise described by a buyer as the thing that impressed them most.
**Use the phrasing: "knowing exactly what we wanted," "picked up the style," "exactly what we got."**

## 🎯 The Quality Gate, validated by a customer's own words

> "The fact that there's a project manager that I can sort of go through is very helpful. They sort of screen the work before I get to it. So in case there's any issues, that's resolved before I get to it. If I'm hiring a freelance editor, **I'm the project manager**, and I have to do all that."
— Ryan Stober

**"If I'm hiring a freelance editor, I'm the project manager"** is the single most useful sentence in this file.
It's the process-overhead insight stated by a buyer, and it's the exact value of Oakhaven's Quality Gate phase.

> "I can send it and then I can move on to other tasks. There's an intuitive editor that's looking at it and paying attention."
— Andrew Cardy
