## The Offer

**What they sell:** Done-for-you short-form and long-form video editing, monthly. The client films, uploads raw footage, gets edited video back in 24 hours. Every account gets two people: a trained lead editor and a project manager who reviews every video before delivery.

**Price:** [GAP] Not stated. The VSL implies $1,250–$2,500/mo by anchoring against "$5,000–$10,000/month in payroll" and claiming "less than one-fourth." That is an inference. Structure (retainer / per-video / credit subscription) also unconfirmed.

Known market prices: VidChops $495 and $995. EditCrew $1,500–$2,450. Oakhaven's implied price sits in EditCrew's band. The payroll anchor is aimed at the wrong comparison — the prospect's alternative is a $495 subscription, not an in-house hire. Confirm the real price before writing any script that mentions value.

**Unique mechanism — the Content Immersion System.** Freelancers and agencies deliver generic edits not because they lack software skill, but because they don't understand what the client *means*. They know the surface topic but not the point being made in each moment, so they fall back on stock b-roll. Oakhaven's editors are trained to understand the message first, then choose the visual that lands it.

1. **Brand Download** — before the editor touches a frame: structured deep-dive into the business, audience, competitors, content style, and reference videos.
2. **Contextual Editing** — every graphic, b-roll clip and text overlay matched to the specific point being made at that moment. Talking about closing $10k deals means showing the money hitting the account, not a generic "graph goes up" animation.
3. **Quality Gate** — the project manager watches every edit before the client sees it, checked against the Brand Download profile: style match, b-roll relevance, typos, pacing.

**Core deliverables:**
- Trained lead editor assigned to the account
- Project manager who reviews every video for storytelling, editing and brand style
- 24-hour turnaround
- Custom graphics instead of stock b-roll
- Free AI bot for viral hooks, included with orders
- 14-day money-back guarantee [GAP — confirm live; the VSL lists it as an option against "unlimited revisions on first project"]
- [GAP] Video volume per month, formats covered, revision policy, onboarding timeline

**Proof assets:**
- Founder runs 3 online brands; the same team edits their content and ads, with the founder's own ad spend behind them
- 1,000+ hours of founder training invested in the team
- "Thousands of reels, YouTube videos and ads" delivered

[GAP — the big one] No named case studies, no before/after pairs, no stats, no screenshots. The VSL marks three places for proof that don't exist: the side-by-side comparison, the first-draft approval rate, and the onboarding screen recording. Produce the side-by-side first: the mechanism is a visual claim with no visual evidence behind it.

**Founder story:** Runs 3 online brands. Knew that without good reels and ads those brands go bankrupt. Tried freelancers — bad edits or got ghosted. Tried agencies — better, but the edits never expressed what he was actually saying. Built and trained his own team instead. Noticed the pattern that became the mechanism: his editors only produced good work once they understood the point he was making *and* knew which visual would land it.

[GAP] Founder's name, years operating (the VSL has an unfilled `[X years]`), team size.

**Known objections.** No sales calls yet, so none of these come from someone deciding on this offer at this price. All are evidenced verbatim — sources in `knowledge-base/research/`. Ranked by difficulty, not frequency:

1. **Understanding can't be outsourced.** *"He doesn't know your audience. Giving your viewers what they want is up to you. You can't expect someone else to tell you that."* Brand Download exists to defeat this. Hardest one.
2. **Loss of ownership.** *"In many ways then I feel like its their video and not mine lol."*
3. **Editors drift to their own style.** *"I have used remote editors and inhouse editors. They all end up changing the edits to match their workflow."*
4. **The brief can't survive the edit.** Editing is where the narrative gets decided; handing over footage means losing that or locking the plan so tight the video goes flat.
5. **Outsourcing degrades the brand.** *"I felt like they weren't going to diminish the quality of my brand."*
6. **Turnaround claims aren't believable.** *"They're gonna give us our first draft in forty-eight hours. That doesn't sound real."*
7. **No price anchor.** $50/video freelancers to $1,000+/video agencies.
8. **Billing trust** (hourly only) — *"I believe the editing will take 5 hours, but what if they say 20?"*
9. **Quality risk from past outsourcing** — generic work, stock assets, typos.
10. **Not ready yet** — *"I don't think I'm ready to hire someone."*

Nobody goes looking for this: *"small businesses think their 'vibe' requires a human who gets it. agencies think their margin depends on looking premium. creators think engagement comes from authenticity. none of them wake up thinking 'i need cheaper video.'"* The ads have to create the problem, not answer a searched-for need.

## ICP

Three groups, all self-filming and posting regularly:
- **Small business owners** — service businesses running short-form and YouTube; often solo
- **Coaches and personal-brand operators** — content is the acquisition channel
- **Content creators** — the bottleneck is editing, not ideas

**Coaches do buy** — five appear in competitor case studies. But every one of them is sold on time reclaimed and money saved, never on charging more. Three of the client's seven planned angles claim coaches buy to raise their prices; nothing in the market supports that. Keep coaches as an ICP, treat "premium editing → premium pricing" as unproven, and let the ICP interviews test it before it gets budget.

**Agencies are the segment that pays most** and the client chose not to pursue them. Worth revisiting. Buyer-by-price-tier evidence is in `knowledge-base/research/voice-of-customer.md`.

[GAP] Geography, current client count, actual buyer mix.

**What they're doing now** — the state determines the angle:
1. Editing it themselves — CapCut, nights and weekends, 8–12 hours per long-form video
2. Cycling through freelancers — trial and error, overspending, getting ghosted, re-explaining the brief
3. Already using an agency — output is "fine" but generic, revision cycles are exhausting
4. Not posting consistently — filming stops at the editing wall

**Triggers:**
- The editing eats the weekend or the hours after the kids are asleep
- A freelancer ghosts or blows a deadline before a launch
- An edit comes back with typos or obviously wrong footage
- Competitors post 2–3 reels a day while they can barely ship one

**What they want, in their words:**
- Film 10 videos in a day, press one button, get them all back edited
- Content that "actually looks and sounds like me"
- Post consistently without editing being the thing that stops them
- "An extension of my team, not just an editor"

**Dominant fear:** they have already paid for editing that came back generic or never came back at all. The default assumption is that Oakhaven is the same thing with better copy.

## Personas

Hypotheses. Built from competitor VOC, not from Oakhaven's own calls or customers. Run Creative Strategy against `knowledge-base/research/` to score and decompose them properly.

**1. The Midnight CapCut Owner**
Runs one or two service businesses, does client work and ops themselves. Editing in CapCut at 11pm; 8–12 hours per video. Pain: editing consumes the hours that should go to revenue. Wants the hours back without the output getting worse. Trigger: a weekend lost to editing. Awareness: Problem-Aware → Solution-Aware.

**2. The Burned Re-Hirer**
Has outsourced before, once or several times. Overspent, got ghosted, or tolerating an editor whose output is "fine but not right." Pain: the revision loop — re-explaining the vision, catching typos, chasing deadlines. Wants one team that doesn't need managing. Trigger: another ghost, another edit that misses the point. Awareness: Solution-Aware → Product-Aware, deeply sceptical.

**3. The Coach**
Coach or consultant using content as the acquisition channel. Posting regularly, edits look basic next to bigger names in the niche. Evidenced pain: time and cost, same as everyone else. Trigger: falling behind on output. Awareness: Problem-Aware.
Note: the "wants to charge more" version of this persona has no supporting VOC. Do not script it until interviews confirm it.

**4. The Blocked Creator**
Ideas and scripts written, filming is fine. *"I can record videos all day, but once it comes to editing, that's where I stop."* Pain: editing is the wall that ends the project. Wants to clear the backlog. Awareness: Solution-Aware, lowest budget confidence of the four — weakest fit for a $1k+/mo offer from cold.

## Voice & Tone

Founder-led, direct, plain-spoken. Peer-to-peer operator talking to another operator — "I run 3 online brands myself," "let's be real," "been there before, right?" Confident about the mechanism without hype. Short sentences. Never corporate-agency.

**Their vocabulary:**
- "Content Immersion System" — the mechanism. [GAP] The dump also says "Method". Pick one.
- "Brand Download," "Contextual Editing," "Quality Gate" — the three phases
- "Project manager" — [GAP] the dump also says "creative manager". Pick one.
- "Custom graphics" against "generic b-roll"
- "Looks and sounds like you"
- "Ghosting," "back-and-forth," "generic edits" — the enemy vocabulary

**Avoid:**
- The AI-slop list in CLAUDE.md rule 3: unlock, revolutionize, elevate, empower, transform, journey, landscape, leverage
- "Unlimited revisions" as a headline — the mechanism says revisions shouldn't be needed; leading with it undercuts that
- Hourly-billing framing
- "Premium" as the central value word until the premium angle is validated

## Positioning

**The one differentiator: comprehension.** Nobody in the market claims their editor understands what you *mean*, not just what you said. The Content Immersion System owns that lane and it is the only defensible position available.

**Everything else is table stakes.** Dedicated PM, two-person team, 24-hour turnaround, 14-day guarantee, "a team that sounds like you," custom graphics, flat pricing, no-ghosting — all verified as already running in competitor ads and landing copy, some of it verbatim. Use them as reassurance after the mechanism lands. Never as a hook. Evidence: `knowledge-base/research/competitors/ad-teardown.md`.

Reposition the PM away from "you get a dedicated project manager" — a commodity at $995 — toward what the Quality Gate catches: the wrong graphic, the irrelevant b-roll, the typo.

**The market is sophisticated.** The client flagged this twice and it's now settled. Straight benefit claims — save 10 hours a week, never miss a deadline, 24-hour turnaround — are exhausted and will get scrolled. **Run VSL v2 (mechanism-led), not VSL v1 (time-saved led).** VSL v1 is, beat for beat, an ad EditCrew and Boomin Brands are both running right now.

Competitors: EditCrew, Boomin Brands Media, The Editors Connection, VidChops, Podcast Monkey. Ad teardown and pricing analysis in `knowledge-base/research/competitors/`.
