# What We Need From You

Answering Part 1 unblocks the KPI model, the media buying playbook, the campaign structure, and the value
argument in every script. Most are one-liners. Part 2 takes longer — start it now, it's the one that matters most.

Where you don't know a number, write "don't know" rather than guessing. A wrong number is worse than a blank one.

---

## Part 1 — The quick answers

> **Pricing & offer**
> 1. What's the price, and how is it structured? Monthly retainer, per-video, or a video-credit subscription?
> 2. What exactly does one month/package include? Number of videos, max length, formats covered (reels / YouTube long-form / ads / podcast clips).
> 3. What's your revision policy — how many rounds, and what's the turnaround on a revision?
> 4. Is the guarantee live, and which one: 14-day money-back, or unlimited revisions on the first project? (Your VSL has both as options.)
> 5. Is there a minimum commitment, or is it month-to-month?
> 6. Average client lifetime so far — how many months does a client typically stay?
> 7. Roughly what does it cost you to deliver one month for one client? (editor time + PM time + tools)
>
> **The funnel**
> 8. Landing page URL — is it live? Same for the VSL.
> 9. Which is the real conversion action: booking the 15-min call, or the "Order Now" direct purchase? If both, which one do you want the ads optimised for?
> 10. What are you using for booking (Calendly / GHL / other) and for CRM?
> 11. Is the Meta Pixel installed? Conversions API? Any third-party attribution (Hyros, TripleWhale, etc.)?
> 12. Which countries are we targeting?
>
> **Team & capacity**
> 13. Monthly ad budget to start.
> 14. Two capacity numbers: (a) how many new clients can you onboard per month without quality dropping, and (b) how many new ad creatives can you produce per week?
> 15. Who's on camera in the ads — you, or an actor? Who handles the inbound calls?
> 16. Your name, how long the team has been running, and team size. (Your VSL has an unfilled "[X years]" placeholder.)

## Part 2 — Proof

The mechanism is a strong claim with nothing attached to it, and this market has already been burned, so buyers
are sceptical by default. Ranked by what moves the needle most:

1. **The side-by-side.** Take one piece of footage. Have someone produce a generic edit of it, and put your
   team's edit next to it. Same segment, same audio. This is the single most valuable asset you can produce —
   your own VSL script marks the spot for it and calls it "your most powerful proof element." Everything you're
   claiming is visual, and right now there's nothing visual proving it.
2. **First-draft approval rate.** What percentage of edits get approved without a revision round? Even a rough
   number from your last 20 deliveries. It proves the Quality Gate directly.
3. **A screen recording of the Brand Download** — your actual onboarding process, however simple. Makes the
   mechanism concrete instead of described.
4. **Any client results with numbers** — views, followers, revenue before and after. Two or three changes the
   whole offer.
5. **Content from your own 3 brands** that this team edits. You already have it, it costs nothing, and it
   substantiates the founder story.

Also, two naming decisions to lock: "Content Immersion **System**" or "**Method**"? And is the second person a
"project manager" or a "creative manager"? Both appear in your notes.

## Part 3 — Sales calls (the one that actually matters)

**Start recording every sales call from your next one.** Fathom's free tier does it automatically on Zoom and
Meet. Send transcripts as they come in — wins and losses both. Losses are usually more useful.

Personas are currently built from competitor reviews and forum threads, because that's all that exists. It's a
reasonable stand-in, but it can't tell us why someone says no to *your* offer at *your* price. At 5 transcripts
the strategy gets rebuilt properly, and it will change.

If you're weeks away from 5 calls, the interim option is 5–8 twenty-minute conversations with people in your
target market who aren't clients yet. Ask: what have you tried for editing, what went wrong, what did it cost
you, what would you pay, what would make you say no to a service like this. Record those.
